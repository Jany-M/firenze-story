# The Uffizi

The "offices" of Florence: a palace Duke Cosimo I de' Medici had Giorgio Vasari build from 1560 to bring the thirteen
magistracies, until then scattered around the city, under one roof. It rose on a long strip between Piazza della Signoria and
the Arno, in a crowded quarter with the Mint (the Zecca), a river port and the Romanesque church of San Pier Scheraggio.
Two long wings face each other across a courtyard only 14 m wide, a street in all but name, and the short Arno wing closes it
with a Doric screen that frames the river.

## What you see
- A ground-floor portico of piers with niches and Doric columns, a mezzanine, the *piano nobile* with windows under alternating
  triangular and curved pediments, and an upper loggia, all in grey *pietra serena* against pale plaster.
- A continuous cornice and unbroken string courses, which stress the length of the street.
- On the Arno side a *serliana* (an arch flanked by lintelled openings) under a loggia, and from 1565 the Corridoio Vasariano
  leaving the building towards Ponte Vecchio and Palazzo Pitti.

## Through time
| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1560 | Vasari starts the building for Cosimo I |
| 1565 | Corridoio Vasariano built in about six months for the wedding of Francesco I; the west and Arno wings stand (it.wikipedia gives this year on one line and 1575 on another) |
| 1572 | The magistracies of the San Pier Scheraggio side have moved in, the building still unfinished |
| 1574–81 | Vasari dies in 1574; Buontalenti and Alfonso Parigi complete it (October 1580 on it.wikipedia, 1581 on en.wikipedia; the Lancaster notes give Vasari's death as 1564, a slip for 1574) |
| 1586 | Buontalenti's Teatro Mediceo in the east wing |
| 1769 | Pietro Leopoldo opens the gallery to the public, with a new entrance by Zanobi del Rosso |
| 1842–56 | 28 statues of famous Tuscans placed in the niches |
| 1865–71 | Florence is capital of Italy: the theatre is adapted for the Senate |

In the app the Uffizi is a clickable model from 1560. Dates of its parts (1565, 1572, 1580, statues 1850) are inferred from the
sources above, which disagree on some years. Details and open points: [technical page](/docs/landmarks/UFFIZI/).
