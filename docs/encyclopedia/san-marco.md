# San Marco

In 1437 Cosimo il Vecchio gave the old Silvestrine monastery on the edge of the city to the Dominicans and paid Michelozzo
to rebuild it. The friars got light, quiet cloisters, a library open to scholars, and cells that Fra Angelico, one of
them, painted with frescoes. Later the convent's most famous prior, Girolamo Savonarola, preached from here against the
Medici. The church in front of it got its present front in 1777–78; the convent became a museum in 1869.

## What you see
- On Piazza San Marco, the church front of 1777–78: two orders of pilasters, statues in niches, a pediment with a relief.
- Beside it, the long plain front of the convent, now the Museo di San Marco.
- Behind the church, the convent's two cloisters (each with its own card in the model).
- Inside the convent (not in the model): Michelozzo's library and the friars' cells with Fra Angelico's frescoes.

## The cloisters
- **Chiostro di Sant'Antonino**, Michelozzo's cloister from before 1440, is named after Antonino Pierozzi, prior of San
  Marco and later archbishop of Florence. Today it is the entrance court of the Museo di San Marco.
- **Chiostro di San Domenico** lies further in, with arches on columns and two floors of shuttered windows above.

## Through time
| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| before 1300 | The Silvestrine monks build a monastery here |
| 1437 | The Dominicans take over; Cosimo il Vecchio pays for the rebuilding |
| 1439–44 | Michelozzo rebuilds convent and church (consecrated at Epiphany 1443) |
| 1512 | The bell tower |
| 1679 | Silvani's tribune and ceiling in the church |
| 1777–78 | The new church front |
| 1866–69 | The convent is confiscated and becomes a museum |
