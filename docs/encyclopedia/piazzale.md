# Piazzale Michelangelo

The terrace over Florence that Giuseppe Poggi built when the city was the capital of Italy (1869), as a monument to
Michelangelo: in its middle the bronze copy of the David (1873) above copies of the four allegories of the Medici Chapel;
above it the Loggia, meant as a museum of his works and a café since 1876; below it the Rampe (1872–76), ramps, grottoes
and fountains climbing from the tower of San Niccolò, whose water runs again since 2019.

## What you see
- The monument: steps with posts and chains, the four bronze allegories, the David
- The balustrade along the terrace's edge over the city
- The Loggia's arcade on paired columns
- The Rampe's walls, grottoes and cascades between the bends of Viale Poggi
