# Santa Maria del Carmine

Carmelite friars who came from Pisa founded their church on the western edge of Oltrarno in 1268. It grew into one of the
great convents of the city, and in the 1420s Masolino and the young Masaccio painted the Brancacci chapel inside it –
frescoes that taught perspective and the human figure to a whole generation of painters. In 1675–83 the Corsini family
added a Baroque chapel for their saint, Andrea Corsini. On the night of 28 January 1771 a fire gutted the church; the two
chapels survived, and Giuseppe Ruggieri and Giulio Mannaioni rebuilt the rest inside the old walls. The front was never
faced and is still bare stone.

## What you see
- On Piazza del Carmine, the rough front of rubble and brick with its tall window and stone portal.
- The long flanks with big round-headed windows, a row of small arches and a plastered band under the eaves.
- Over the crossing, the plastered octagon with its low roof; at the end of the left transept, the Corsini chapel's drum and lantern.
- On the west side, the bell gable with its two bells.
- In the convent, the first cloister with an open loggia above its arches (its own card in the model). The Brancacci
  chapel is entered from here, at the end of the right transept; it has no outside of its own.

## The Corsini chapel
Pier Francesco Silvani built it in 1675–83 for Saint Andrea Corsini, a Carmelite of this convent who became bishop of
Fiesole: marble, stucco and Luca Giordano's painted dome inside, an octagonal drum and lantern outside. It is one of the
few pieces of Roman Baroque in Florence, and it came through the fire of 1771.

## The first cloister
Rebuilt from 6 March 1597: the south loggia was finished by September 1600, paid for in part by Giovanni Battista
Michelozzi, whose arms are on its parapet; the other three loggias followed in 1612. Arches on pietra serena columns
below, an open loggia above.

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1268 | The Carmelites from Pisa found the church |
| 1423–28 | Masolino and Masaccio paint the Brancacci chapel; Filippino Lippi finishes it in the 1480s |
| 1476 | The church finished |
| 1597–1612 | The first cloister rebuilt |
| 1675–83 | The Corsini chapel |
| 1771 | The fire destroys the inside of the church |
| 1775–82 | Ruggieri and Mannaioni rebuild it; the front stays bare |
