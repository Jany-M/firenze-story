# Orsanmichele

The name comes from San Michele in Orto, a church in a kitchen garden. On its site the commune built an open market
loggia for grain, then a stone hall with two storeys of granary above it. The guilds decorated its outer niches with
statues of their patron saints by Ghiberti, Donatello, Verrocchio and Giambologna.

## Through time

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1290 | Arnolfo di Cambio (attr.) builds an open loggia for the grain market. A miraculous image of the Madonna is venerated there. |
| 1304 | The loggia burns. |
| 1337 | The new building is begun by Francesco Talenti, Neri di Fioravante and Benci di Cione. It has a market hall below and granaries above. Each of the guilds receives a niche on the outside. |
| 1349–59 | Andrea Orcagna's marble tabernacle for the Madonna by Bernardo Daddi. |
| 1380 | The arches are walled up with Gothic tracery, and the hall becomes a church. |
| c. 1404 | The granary floors are finished. Grain is poured down through chutes in the piers, which can still be seen. |
| 1412–1601 | The guilds' statues: Ghiberti's St John the Baptist (1412–16), Donatello's St Mark and St George (1411–17), Verrocchio's Doubting Thomas (1467–83), Giambologna's St Luke (1601). |
| 1569 | Buontalenti's covered passage to the Palazzo dell'Arte della Lana. |
