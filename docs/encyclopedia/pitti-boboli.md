# Palazzo Pitti and the Boboli Garden

The banker Luca Pitti began his palace across the Arno in 1458, a block of rough stone seven windows wide, larger than
anything the Medici had built. A century later Eleonora di Toledo, Duke Cosimo I's wife, bought it, and it became the
grand dukes' home for three hundred years. Ammannati gave it its courtyard and garden wings, the Parigi stretched the front
to twenty-three windows, and the Lorraine added the two wings that close the piazza. Behind it the Boboli garden climbs the
hill to Forte Belvedere.

## What you see
- A front about 200 m long in huge rough blocks of pietra forte, with tall arched windows: three storeys in the middle,
  two at the ends, and a balcony running along each floor.
- Ammannati's courtyard behind the main door, its columns banded with rough stones, opening onto the garden.
- In the garden: the amphitheatre, dug out where stone for the palace was quarried, with stone tiers, statues in niches
  and an Egyptian obelisk in the middle; Buontalenti's grotto by the corridor's exit; the Kaffeehaus on the hill; the
  Neptune basin.
- At the top, Forte Belvedere, a star-shaped fortress Buontalenti built in 1590–91, with an older white villa inside.

## Through time
| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1458 | Luca Pitti begins the palace (work stops in 1465) |
| 1549–50 | Eleonora di Toledo buys it; Tribolo starts the garden |
| 1558–70 | Ammannati: courtyard, garden wings, the side doors become windows |
| 1565 | The Vasari Corridor links the palace with Palazzo Vecchio |
| 1583–93 | Buontalenti's grotto |
| 1590–91 | Forte Belvedere |
| 1599 | Stone tiers in the amphitheatre |
| 1618–1640s | The Parigi lengthen the front to 23 windows |
| 1637 | The amphitheatre, with statues and urns, is inaugurated |
| 1764–66 | The south wing on the piazza (rondò delle Carrozze) |
| 1776 | The Kaffeehaus; the Palazzina della Meridiana begun |
| 1777–78 | The Neptune basin |
| 1790 | The obelisk in the amphitheatre |
| 1865–71 | Royal residence while Florence is capital of Italy |
| 1919 | Vittorio Emanuele III gives the palace to the State |

In the app the palace appears in 1458 and grows phase by phase; the garden pieces and the fort appear in their years. Details
and open points: [technical page](/docs/landmarks/PITTI-BOBOLI/).
