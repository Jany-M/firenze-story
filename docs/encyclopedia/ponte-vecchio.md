# Ponte Vecchio

The oldest bridge in Florence, at the river's narrowest point, near where the Roman road crossed the Arno. The present
bridge was rebuilt after the flood of 1333. It has three segmental arches and shops on both sides, and Vasari's corridor
runs over the top.

## Through time

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| Roman times | A bridge near this place carries the Via Cassia over the Arno (its exact site and form are uncertain). |
| 1117, 1333 | Floods carry the bridge away. The 1333 flood takes Ponte Vecchio, Carraia and Santa Trinita. |
| 1345 | Rebuilt in stone, attributed to Neri di Fioravante (Vasari says Taddeo Gaddi). An inscription on the bridge records the date. |
| 1565 | Vasari builds the corridor for Cosimo I in five months. It joins Palazzo Vecchio and the Uffizi to Palazzo Pitti. |
| 1593 | Ferdinando I moves the goldsmiths onto the bridge in place of the butchers and tanners. |
| 17th c. | The shops grow back rooms, the *retrobotteghe*, which hang over the river on timber struts. |
| 1901 | The bust of Benvenuto Cellini by Raffaello Romanelli is set on the central terrace. |
| 3–4 Aug 1944 | The retreating German army blows up every other bridge. Ponte Vecchio is spared, but the houses at both ends are mined to block it. |
| 4 Nov 1966 | The flood fills the shops and almost overtops the bridge. |

## In the app

The bridge of 1345 has its shops. The corridor arrives in 1565 and the back rooms around 1600. Earlier bridges are drawn
from their dated specs. How it is built: [landmark notes](/docs/landmarks/PONTE-VECCHIO/).
