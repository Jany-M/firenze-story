# The Bargello

The oldest public palace of Florence. It was built from 1255 for the Capitano del Popolo, and from 1261 it was the seat
of the Podestà. From 1574 it housed the Bargello (the chief of police) and the prisons, and since 1865 it has been the
Museo Nazionale, with Donatello, Michelangelo, Cellini and the Della Robbia.

## Through time

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1255 | The palace is begun on Via del Proconsolo. It takes in the Boscoli tower, later called the Volognana. |
| 1280–85 | The courtyard with its porticoes. |
| 1316–1320 | The wings on Via Ghibellina, Via dell'Acqua and Via della Vigna Vecchia; the *Verone*, the loggia over the portico (1317–20). |
| 1340–45 | Neri di Fioravante raises the palace and builds the vaulted hall of the General Council, today's Donatello hall. |
| 1345–67 | The open staircase from the courtyard to the Verone. |
| 1574 | Seat of the Bargello and the prisons. Executions take place in the courtyard until 1786, when Grand Duke Pietro Leopoldo abolishes the death penalty. |
| 1857–1865 | Restored, and opened as the national museum of sculpture. |
