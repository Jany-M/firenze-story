# Loggia del Pesce

When Cosimo I had the Vasari Corridor built along the Arno, the fishmongers who sold by the Ponte Vecchio had to go. Vasari
built them a new loggia in the Mercato Vecchio, the old market in the heart of the city, in 1568–69: arches on grey stone
columns, with round plaques of fish and sea creatures. When the old centre was torn down at the end of the 19th century,
the loggia was taken apart and its pieces stored at San Marco. In 1955 it was put back up in Piazza dei Ciompi, where the
flea market is held.

## What you see
- In Piazza dei Ciompi, a long loggia of nine arches a side on slender columns, under a tiled roof.
- Round medallions with fish and sea scenes between the arches, the Medici arms at the corners.
- In old photographs of the Mercato Vecchio, the same loggia full of stalls and awnings, the market's houses close around it.

## Through time
| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1568–69 | Vasari builds the loggia for Cosimo I in the Mercato Vecchio |
| 1699 | One arch is added at each end |
| 1889 | It is taken apart in the demolition of the old centre; pieces go to San Marco |
| 1955 | It is rebuilt in Piazza dei Ciompi |

In the app the loggia stands in the Mercato Vecchio from 1569 to 1889 and in Piazza dei Ciompi from 1955. Details and open
points: [technical page](/docs/landmarks/LOGGIA-DEL-PESCE/).
