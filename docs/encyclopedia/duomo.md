# Santa Maria del Fiore (the Duomo)

The cathedral of Florence stands on the site of the old church of Santa Reparata, which was taken down bit by bit as the
new building rose around it. It was designed by Arnolfo di Cambio, enlarged by Francesco Talenti, and crowned by Filippo
Brunelleschi's dome, the largest masonry dome ever built.

## Through time

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 8 Sept 1296 | First stone laid by the cardinal legate. Arnolfo di Cambio is the architect. |
| 1302–1331 | After Arnolfo's death the works slow down. The lower part of his façade is built and decorated with statues. |
| 1357–1380 | Francesco Talenti takes over and enlarges the plan: wider nave bays and the vaults, closed around 1378–80. |
| 1380–1421 | The octagon and the three tribunes. The drum is finished in 1410–13. |
| 1418–1420 | The competition for the dome is won by Brunelleschi (with Ghiberti at first as co-master). |
| 1420–1436 | The dome is built without centring: two shells, herring-bone brickwork, stone and timber chains. Pope Eugene IV consecrates the cathedral on 25 March 1436. |
| 1439–1445 | The four *tribune morte* (exedrae) around the drum. |
| 1446–1461 | The lantern, begun to Brunelleschi's design and finished after his death. |
| 1471 | Andrea del Verrocchio's gilded copper ball and cross are hoisted onto the lantern. |
| 1507–1515 | Baccio d'Agnolo builds one face of the gallery (*ballatoio*) at the base of the dome. It is left unfinished after criticism, reportedly Michelangelo's "cage for crickets". |
| 1572–79 | Vasari and Federico Zuccari fresco the Last Judgement inside the dome. |
| 1587 | Arnolfo's unfinished façade is pulled down. For three centuries the front stays bare, painted only for festivities. |
| 1876–1887 | The present marble façade by Emilio De Fabris (white Carrara, green Prato, red Maremma). |

## Things to look for

- The marble revetment of white, green and red panels was added years after each part was built. Before that, the walls
  were bare brick and stone.
- Paolo Uccello's clock (1443) on the inside of the façade runs anticlockwise and counts the hours of the day from sunset.
- In 1465 Domenico di Michelino painted Dante holding the Divine Comedy, with the newly domed cathedral behind him (inside the church).

## In the app

The cathedral rises part by part: the nave bays, the tribunes, the drum, the dome in three stages, the lantern and the ball.
Each part is first shown in bare brick, then with its marble. It has Arnolfo's façade until 1587, a bare front until
1887, and then the façade of today. How it is built: [landmark notes](/docs/landmarks/DUOMO/).
