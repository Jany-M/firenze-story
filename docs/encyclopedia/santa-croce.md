# Santa Croce

The great church of the Franciscans, built on the marshy ground east of the old city. It holds the tombs of
Michelangelo, Galileo, Machiavelli and Rossini, the Giotto chapels, and Brunelleschi's Pazzi Chapel. Its piazza was the
field of the *calcio* matches.

## Through time

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| c. 1228 | A first, smaller Franciscan church. |
| 1294–95 | The new church is begun from the east end, attributed to Arnolfo di Cambio. |
| c. 1320–25 | Giotto paints the Bardi and Peruzzi chapels. |
| c. 1385 | The nave is finished. The church is consecrated in 1443. |
| c. 1429–1470 | The Pazzi Chapel in the first cloister, designed by Brunelleschi. |
| 1530 | During the siege, the defenders play a match of calcio in the piazza to show their contempt for the besiegers. |
| 1842–1865 | Gaetano Baccani's bell tower. |
| 1857–1863 | Niccolò Matas's marble façade, paid for in part by the Englishman Francis Sloane. |
| 4 Nov 1966 | The flood: the water stands about 5 m deep in the piazza and floods the church and the museum, ruining Cimabue's Crucifix. |

## In the app

The church is built westwards from 1300 to 1385 and keeps a rough façade until 1863, when the marble façade and the bell
tower arrive. How it is built: [landmark notes](/docs/landmarks/SANTA-CROCE/).
