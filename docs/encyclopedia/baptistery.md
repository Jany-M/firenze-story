# The Baptistery of San Giovanni

An octagon clad in white and green marble, consecrated in 1059. Medieval Florentines believed it was a Roman temple of
Mars. Generations of Florentines, Dante among them, were baptised here, and its three bronze doors mark the start of
Renaissance sculpture.

## Through time

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 6 Nov 1059 | Consecrated by Pope Nicholas II, formerly bishop of Florence. |
| 1059–1128 | The marble revetment of the outside. The roof pyramid is finished in 1128. |
| c. 1150 | The lantern. |
| 1202 | The rectangular *scarsella*, the apse on the west side. |
| from 1225 | The mosaics of the dome: the Last Judgement and stories of Genesis, Joseph, Christ and the Baptist. |
| 1330–1336 | Andrea Pisano's bronze doors, today on the south side. |
| 1401–1424 | Lorenzo Ghiberti wins the competition of 1401 against Brunelleschi and makes the north doors. |
| 1425–1452 | Ghiberti's east doors, gilded, which Michelangelo called the "Gates of Paradise". The originals are now in the Museo dell'Opera. |
| 1502–1571 | Bronze groups over the doors: Rustici (1506–11), Sansovino (1502–05) and Vincenzo Danti (1569–71). |
