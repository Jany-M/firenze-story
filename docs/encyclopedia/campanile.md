# Giotto's Campanile

The cathedral's bell tower is almost 85 m high and was clad in white, green and red marble as it rose. It was begun by
Giotto, continued by Andrea Pisano, and finished by Francesco Talenti, who left out the spire that had been planned.

## Through time

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 18 July 1334 | Giotto, master of the cathedral works, lays the first stone. He dies in 1337 with the base storey done. |
| 1337–1348 | Andrea Pisano builds the second storey with its niches for statues. He and his workshop carve the hexagonal reliefs of the base: the Creation, the arts, the sciences and the trades. |
| 1350–1359 | Francesco Talenti builds the three upper storeys with their tall bifore and trifore windows, and the projecting crown. |
| 1437–39 | Luca della Robbia adds five reliefs on the north side (grammar, dialectic, music, geometry and arithmetic). |
| 1415–1436 | Statues of prophets and sibyls for the niches, by Donatello and Nanni di Bartolo among others. The originals are now in the Museo dell'Opera. |

## Things to look for

- The lower band of hexagons tells the story of humankind through its work. The lozenges above show the planets, the
  virtues, the liberal arts and the sacraments.
- The windows get larger storey by storey. This makes the tower look lighter towards the top.
