# Loggia dei Lanzi

The Republic's open-air hall for its ceremonies, also called the Loggia della Signoria. It is named after the German
landsknechts (*Lanzi*) of Cosimo I's guard, who were quartered here. Since the 16th century it has been a gallery of sculpture.

## Through time

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1376–1382 | Built by Benci di Cione and Simone Talenti: three great round arches on clustered piers. |
| 1384–89 | The Virtues in quatrefoils between the arches, from designs by Agnolo Gaddi. |
| 1554 | Benvenuto Cellini's bronze Perseus with the head of Medusa, under the left arch. |
| 1583 | Giambologna's Rape of the Sabines, under the right arch, carved from a single block. |
| c. 1585 | The roof becomes a terrace with a balustrade, linked to the Uffizi works. |
| 1789 | The two Medici lions, one Roman and one by Flaminio Vacca (1598), are brought from the Villa Medici in Rome. |
| 19th c. | Menelaus with the body of Patroclus, Giambologna's Hercules and Nessus (made in 1599), and Pio Fedi's Rape of Polyxena (1865). |

## In the app

The loggia appears in 1376, and the Virtues, the terrace and each statue appear on the date it arrived. Donatello's
Judith stands here from 1506 to 1582. How it is built: [landmark notes](/docs/landmarks/LOGGIA-DEI-LANZI/).
