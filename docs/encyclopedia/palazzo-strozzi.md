# Palazzo Strozzi

Filippo Strozzi, a banker back from exile, spent twenty years buying and pulling down the houses on the corner of Via
Tornabuoni before laying the first stone of his palace on 6 August 1489, on a day his astrologers chose. He died two years
later. Cronaca carried the work on and crowned it with the great cornice in 1502, but the family ran out of money and luck:
in 1538 the works stopped for good and Cosimo I confiscated the building. One side and half a cornice were never finished.

## What you see
- A free-standing block in rough pietra forte, the stones bolder at the bottom and flatter towards the top.
- Small barred windows high on the ground floor, two rows of arched double windows above, a round-arched door in the
  middle of each street front.
- Cronaca's huge cornice along the top – on the north and east sides, and on half of the west side.
- At the corners, the wrought-iron lanterns by Niccolò Grosso, called il Caparra, with rings and torch holders along the walls.
- A stone bench running along the base, added in the 1860s.
- Inside, a courtyard with arches on grey stone columns.
- The south side, towards the little lane, still unfinished.

## Through time
| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1466 | Filippo Strozzi returns to Florence and starts buying the houses on the site |
| 1489 | The first stone (6 August); Giuliano da Sangallo makes a wooden model |
| 1490–1504 | Cronaca directs the works |
| 1491–98 | Caparra's ironwork |
| 1502 | The top floor and the cornice |
| 1503 | The courtyard |
| 1538 | Works stop for good; Cosimo I confiscates the palace |
| 1863–65 | Giuseppe Poggi's restoration and the stone bench |
| 1938–40 | The missing half of the Via Tornabuoni cornice is copied from the other half |

In the app the palace appears in 1489 and rises in steps to the cornice of 1502; the last stretch of cornice appears in 1938.
