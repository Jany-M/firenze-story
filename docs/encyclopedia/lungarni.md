# The lungarni

The streets along the Arno stand on high stone walls with a low parapet on top, the *spallette* Florentines lean on to look
at the river. The oldest, on the right bank between the Ponte Vecchio and the Ponte alle Grazie, go back to the 1280s and
1290s. Most of the others came with Giuseppe Poggi's works for Florence as capital, from 1865: the Lungarno della Zecca
Vecchia was built out into the river bed, the Lungarno Serristori over the Renai's mill race, and the Lungarno Torrigiani in
place of the houses of Via de' Bardi that stood in the water. On 4 November 1966 the Arno came over the parapets before dawn
and broke them on the Lungarno Corsini; on 25 May 2016 a burst water main brought down 200 m of the Lungarno Torrigiani's road.

## What you see
- The wall in squared stone, the brick parapet with its stone coping, the road on top.
- Each stretch appears in the year its wall was built; Borgo San Jacopo has houses in the river and no wall.
- 1966–68: a gap in the Lungarno Corsini's parapet. 2016–17: the Lungarno Torrigiani's broken wall top.
- In the Events tab, 4 November 1966: the flood rising through the streets hour by hour.
