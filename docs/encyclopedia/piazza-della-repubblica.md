# Piazza della Repubblica

For centuries this was the Mercato Vecchio, the old market on the site of the Roman forum, with the Ghetto behind it. In
the 1880s and 1890s the city tore the whole quarter down – churches, towers, guild halls and all – and laid out a wide
square for the new capital of Italy. On its west side Vincenzo Micheli built a long palace with a portico and, in the
middle, a triumphal arch over Via degli Strozzi. Its inscription says the old centre of the city had been "restored from
age-old squalor to new life"; many Florentines never forgave it.

## What you see
- On the west side, the Palazzo dell'Arcone: a portico along the whole square and three floors of windows above it.
- In the middle, the great arch over Via degli Strozzi, between paired columns, with the date MDCCCXCV in the frieze, the
  inscription in the attic and four statues of the arts on top.
- In front of it, the Colonna dell'Abbondanza, the column of the old market, put back in 1956.
- Around the square, the cafés: Paszkowski and Gilli on the north side, the Giubbe Rosse on the south.

## Through time
| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1431 | The Colonna dell'Abbondanza is set up in the Mercato Vecchio |
| 1885–95 | The Mercato Vecchio and the Ghetto are demolished |
| 1890 | The square opens as Piazza Vittorio Emanuele II, with the king's equestrian statue (20 September) |
| 1895 | Micheli's Palazzo dell'Arcone |
| 1904 | The statue group of Italy with Art and Science is taken off the arch |
| 1932 | The king's statue is moved to the Cascine |
| 1947 | The square is renamed Piazza della Repubblica |
| 1956 | The Colonna dell'Abbondanza is put back |

In the app the arch building appears in 1895, with its crowning group until 1904. Details and open points:
[technical page](/docs/landmarks/REPUBBLICA/).
