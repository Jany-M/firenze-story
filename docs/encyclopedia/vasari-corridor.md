# The Vasari Corridor

A covered walkway about 750 m long that lets the Medici walk from Palazzo Vecchio, the seat of government, to their home at
Palazzo Pitti without setting foot in the street. Duke Cosimo I had Giorgio Vasari build it in 1565, in nine months, for the
wedding of his son Francesco to Joanna of Austria. It crosses Via della Ninna on an arch, runs along the top floor of the
Uffizi, comes down to a loggia on the Lungarno degli Archibusieri, crosses the Arno above the shops of Ponte Vecchio,
turns round Torre dei Mannelli on stone brackets, passes over the portico of Santa Felicita and reaches the Boboli garden.

## What you see
- A long, plain gallery in pale plaster under a tiled roof, with stone window frames: rectangular windows towards the river,
  round ones towards the city.
- Along the Arno, between the Uffizi and the bridge, the corridor stands on a row of tall round arches, with a walkway beneath.
- At the south end of the bridge, the Mannelli family refused to let their tower be cut through, so the corridor goes round
  it on big stone brackets.
- At Santa Felicita it rests on the church's portico; inside, a window with a heavy grille let the grand-ducal family hear
  Mass unseen.
- Near Boboli it runs on top of a wall, its last stretch carried on a row of brackets.

## Through time
| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1249 | Torre dei Mannelli is mentioned as "new" |
| 1565 | Built between 12 March and 17 December for the Medici wedding (window frames fitted in 1568) |
| 1572 | The loggia on the Lungarno is closed in and let as shops, with rooms hanging over the river |
| 1589 | The window into Santa Felicita |
| 1860 | A terrace opens over the middle of Ponte Vecchio for King Vittorio Emanuele II |
| 1864–85 | After the flood the overhanging shops are cleared away |
| 1944 | 4 August: the German mines destroy the houses at both ends of the bridge; the stretch over Via de' Bardi falls |
| 1950 | The Via de' Bardi stretch is rebuilt with a wider arch |
| 1973 | The corridor reopens (1974 with the collection of self-portraits) |
| 1993 | The Via dei Georgofili bomb damages the Uffizi stretch |
| 2024 | Reopened on 21 December after eight years of work |

In the app the corridor is a model from 1565 (the stretch over the bridge belongs to the Ponte Vecchio model). The shops on
the Lungarno show until 1885, the Via de' Bardi stretch is missing from August 1944 to 1950, and Torre dei Mannelli stands
from 1249. Details and open points: [technical page](/docs/landmarks/CORRIDOIO-VASARIANO/).
