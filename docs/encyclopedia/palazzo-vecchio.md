# Palazzo Vecchio

The palace of the Priors, who governed the Republic, and later of Cosimo I de' Medici. It has been the town hall since
1872. Arnolfo di Cambio's fortress-like block and its 94 m tower still dominate Piazza della Signoria.

## Through time

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1299 | The commune begins the Palazzo dei Priori, attributed to Arnolfo di Cambio. It was built on land of the Uberti, Ghibellines whose houses had been razed. |
| c. 1310–14 | The block is finished, with its gallery on corbels and square (Guelph) merlons. The tower is set on the old Torre dei Foraboschi ("della Vacca") and has swallow-tailed (Ghibelline) merlons. |
| 1323 | The *arengario*, the platform along the façade from which the government spoke to the people. |
| 1343 | The Duke of Athens, briefly lord of Florence, fortifies the palace and is driven out on St Anne's day. |
| 1494–98 | Cronaca builds the Salone dei Cinquecento for the Great Council of Savonarola's republic. |
| 23 May 1498 | Savonarola is hanged and burnt in the piazza in front of the palace. |
| 1504 | Michelangelo's David is set beside the door (moved to the Accademia in 1873; a copy stands there since 1910). |
| 1540 | Cosimo I moves in. Vasari remodels the interior, fills the Salone with frescoes of the wars against Pisa and Siena, and later builds the corridor (1565). |
| 1588–96 | Buontalenti finishes the rear wings towards Via dei Leoni. |
| 1865–71 | Seat of the Chamber of Deputies while Florence is capital of Italy. |
