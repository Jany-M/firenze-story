# San Miniato al Monte

Minias, the city's first martyr, was buried on the hill south of the river; in 1018 the bishop began a church over his grave
for a Benedictine abbey. Its front of white marble and green serpentine, finished in the early 13th century, is one of the
finest pieces of Florentine Romanesque, and you can see it from the whole city. The Arte di Calimala, the cloth merchants'
guild, looked after the church from 1288 and put its eagle on the top. Beside it stand the Bishops' Palace of 1295, the
monastery with its cloister, and Baccio d'Agnolo's bell tower of 1523, never finished – during the siege of 1529–30 it
carried cannon, and Michelangelo had it wrapped in mattresses against the imperial guns.

## What you see
- The marble front: five arches below, the mosaic of Christ between the Virgin and St Minias above, the eagle on the top.
- To the right, the Bishops' Palace and the monastery; behind, the apse and the square bell tower.
- Inside the monastery, the cloister (its own card in the model).
- Around the church, the cemetery of the Porte Sante and the walls of the fortress (city model).

## Through time
| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1018 | The church begun over the grave of St Minias |
| early 13th century | The marble front finished; the mosaic later in the century |
| 1288 | The Arte di Calimala takes the church in its care |
| 1295 | The Bishops' Palace |
| 1443–c. 1455 | The cloister |
| 1499 | The old bell tower falls |
| 1523 | Baccio d'Agnolo's new bell tower, left unfinished |
| 1529–30 | The siege: the tower carries cannon, wrapped in mattresses |
