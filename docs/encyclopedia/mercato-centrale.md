# Mercato Centrale

When Florence became the capital of Italy, the city wanted a modern covered market in place of the stalls around San
Lorenzo. Giuseppe Mengoni, the architect of Milan's Galleria Vittorio Emanuele, built it between 1870 and 1874: a stone
base with tall arches, and above it halls of iron and glass. It opened in 1874 with the International Horticultural
Exhibition and is still the city's food market.

## What you see
- A ground storey of rough stone with tall round arches on every side, green shutters and red iron fans in the arches.
- Above it, the central hall of red cast iron and glass, with glazed gables at the two ends.
- All around, the stalls of the San Lorenzo street market.
- Inside: the food market on the ground floor and, since 2014, the food hall on the first floor.

## Through time
| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1870–74 | Giuseppe Mengoni builds the market |
| 1874 | It opens with the International Horticultural Exhibition |
| 1976–79 | A first floor is built inside on a great lattice beam; an underground car park |
| 2014 | The first floor reopens as a food hall |

In the app the market appears in 1874. Details and open points: [technical page](/docs/landmarks/MERCATO-CENTRALE/).
