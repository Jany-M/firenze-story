# Palazzo Medici Riccardi

The house of the Medici on Via Larga (today Via Cavour), begun around 1444 for Cosimo il Vecchio by Michelozzo after
Cosimo had turned down a grander plan by Brunelleschi. Lorenzo il Magnifico grew up and lived here. Its stone front set
the model for the Florentine Renaissance palace: three storeys of rustication growing smoother towards the top, rows of
round-arched windows, and a great cornice. The Riccardi bought it in 1659 and lengthened it along Via Larga.

## What you see
- On the ground floor bold rusticated blocks of pietra forte; on the piano nobile flatter blocks; on the top floor smooth
  stone, with string courses between them and the projecting cornice above.
- Two-light round-arched windows (*bifore*) on both upper floors: ten bays of Michelozzo's front on Via Larga, seven more
  added by the Riccardi in the same style, and nine on Via de' Gori.
- At the corner, the kneeling windows on consoles that closed the open loggia in 1517, attributed to Michelangelo.
- Inside, an arcaded courtyard modelled on Brunelleschi's Innocenti loggia, and the garden behind a battlemented wall.

## Through time
| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| c. 1444 | Cosimo il Vecchio commissions Michelozzo |
| 1452 / 1460 | The palace is finished (the sources give both years) |
| 1517 | The corner loggia is walled up with the kneeling windows |
| 1659 | Ferdinando II sells the palace to Marchese Gabriello Riccardi for 40,000 scudi (28 March) |
| 1670s–1689 | The Riccardi extend it northwards along Via Larga by seven bays (Ferdinando Tacca, Pier Maria Baldi, Giovan Battista Foggini) |
| 1685 | Luca Giordano paints the ceiling of the Riccardi gallery |
| 1810 | Sold to the grand-ducal government |
| 1874 | Bought by the Provincia di Firenze; today seat of the Prefettura and the Metropolitan City |
