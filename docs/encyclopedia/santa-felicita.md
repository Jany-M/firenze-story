# Santa Felicita

After San Lorenzo, Santa Felicita is probably the oldest church in Florence: Christians were buried here, by the road south
out of the Roman town, in the early 5th century. A Benedictine nunnery grew up round it. In 1565 Vasari's corridor was carried
across its front, and the Medici could hear Mass from a box inside the church without leaving their passage between the
Uffizi and Palazzo Pitti. In 1736–39 Ferdinando Ruggieri rebuilt the church as a single nave for the nuns; Pontormo's
Deposition in the Capponi chapel, by the door, survived from the old church.

## What you see
- The front stands behind the corridor's portico; above the corridor, plaster between grey stone pilasters, two round
  windows and a pediment.
- The long ochre flank with tall windows, and the bare stone bell tower behind the choir.
- In the piazza, the granite column (its own card in the model).

## The column
A granite column with a Corinthian capital of the late 14th century, raised in memory of the victory over the Patarine
heretics fought here in 1244. A glazed terracotta St Peter Martyr stood on it from 1484 until it fell in August 1723; the
statue that replaced it was taken down in the 19th century, and the column has stood bare since.

## Through time
| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| c. 400 | Christian burials and a cemetery church by the Roman road |
| 11th century | A new church; a Benedictine nunnery beside it |
| 1244 | The battle with the Patarines in the piazza |
| 1565 | Vasari's corridor crosses the front |
| 1736–39 | Ferdinando Ruggieri rebuilds the church |
| 1808–10 | The nunnery is suppressed; the church becomes a parish |
